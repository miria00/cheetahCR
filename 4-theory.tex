\section{Modeling Theory and Containment}
\label{sec:theory}\label{app:containment}
The following statements concern represented feasible sets. They do not imply
that a numerical solver has optimized the complete master or that arbitrary
rounding preserves feasibility.

\subsection{Bounded Products and Epigraphs}\label{app:bounded_products}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:mccormick_exact}
For $0\le y,p\le1$, the inequalities $0\le w$, $w\le y$, $w\le p$,
and $w\ge y+p-1$ describe the convex hull of the graph $w=yp$.
If either factor is integral, these inequalities force $w=yp$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The lower envelope is $\max(0,y+p-1)$ and the upper envelope is
$\min(y,p)$~\citep{mccormick1976computability}. For $y=0$ they both force
$w=0$; for $y=1$ they force $w=p$. The cases for integral $p$ are symmetric.
\end{proof}
This is an individual-product property, not a convex-hull theorem for the
complete program. For example,
\begin{equation}
y_1=y_2=p=\tfrac12,\quad w_1=w_2=\tfrac12
\label{eq:rlt_counterexample}
\end{equation}
satisfies the separate envelopes and $y_1+y_2=1$, but violates the identity
$w_1+w_2=p$ that holds for exact products. Linking rows
\begin{equation}
\sum_c w_{ick}=p_{\mathrm{assoc}(i,k)}
\label{eq:rlt_linking}
\end{equation}
can strengthen a branch when its intended integral semantics justify them~\citep{sherali1990hierarchy}.
They must not be added to an immutable compatibility branch while claiming
containment of every original fractional point.

\begin{corollary}[Disaggregated service epigraphs]\label{cor:v3_hull}
For fixed resource costs $a_c,b_c$, the one-choice epigraph has the perspective
form $\sum_c y_c=1$, $y_c\ge0$, $t_c\ge a_cy_c$, $t_c\ge b_cy_c$,
$T\ge\sum_ct_c$. It gives $T\ge\sum_c y_c\max(a_c,b_c)$ and dominates
$T\ge\max(\sum_ca_cy_c,\sum_cb_cy_c)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For each $c$, minimizing $t_c$ gives $y_c\max(a_c,b_c)$. Summing maxima is
at least either sum separately. With a one-hot choice the two formulations
coincide. For shared variable hardware, every additional coupling must also
be disaggregated consistently, and separate product envelopes alone do not prove
the convex hull of the entire HW/SW problem.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Containment of Lifted Sequential Fusion}
\label{app:fast_containment}
This subsection concerns the retained joint tiling--placement block of
Section~\ref{sec:master_lp}, whose coordinates are the tile selection
$y_{ic}$, activation residency $p_{et}$, weight residency $p^W_{it}$,
rematerialization $r_{it}$, the one-hot global-buffer selector
$h_{\mathrm{GM}}\in[0,1]^K$ over a discrete grid $g_1,\ldots,g_K$, and the
per-operation latencies $T_i$ that the objective sums. Write that block's
feasible set at a fixed chip $H$ and workload graph $G$ as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal P_V(H,G)=\{x=(y,p,p^W,r,h_{\mathrm{GM}},T):A_Vx\le b_V(H,G)\},
\label{eq:pv}
\end{equation}
with $\sum_cy_{ic}=1$, $\sum_kh_{\mathrm{GM},k}=1$, all selection and residency
coordinates in $[0,1]$, $T\in\mathbb R^L_{\ge0}$, and Timeloop-derived constants
$T^{\min}_i(c),T^{\max}_i(c),t^k_i(c)$ at $C$ Pareto tile configurations per
operation.

\paragraph{The lifted sequential fusion set.}
A sequential co-design pipeline runs the mapper first, commits to one tile
choice $c^{\mathrm{S}}_i$ per operation, and only then solves a fusion problem
over tensor pinning at a fixed buffer split, without rematerialization. Any
schedule of this shape belongs to the class, whichever system produced it.
FAST~\citep{zhang_fast} is the member whose constants we match in
Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. Of the systems in
Table~\ref{tab:capability-matrix}, the per-op mappers Timeloop and CoSA fit
the class with an empty fusion pattern, and the fusion-aware mappers DeFiNES,
Fast-and-Fusiest and Gemini fit it on the schedules where their fusion stage
consumes committed tiles and their partial placement and recomputation
features are off. To compare a pipeline with CHEETAH in one variable space we
lift the pipeline's feasible set by encoding its committed mapper choice as a
singleton one-hot,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal F^{\mathrm{lift}}_S(H,G)=\Bigl\{(y,p,p^W,T):\ &
y_{i,c^{\mathrm{S}}_i}=1,\ y_{ic}=0\ \forall c\ne c^{\mathrm{S}}_i,\\
&\text{$p$ in the pipeline's consecutive fusion pattern},\ A_Sx\le b_S(H,G)\Bigr\},
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:fflift}
\end{equation}
where $A_S,b_S$ encode the pipeline's buffer capacity at its fixed $g$, its
bandwidth, and its per-stage compute floor under the fixed-$y$ projection.
The pipeline commits to $r=0$ and to that fixed $g$ by construction. The first
$LC+ET+LT$ columns of $A_V$ coincide with $A_S$, and both use the same
bandwidth $b$ and the same mapper cost vectors.

\begin{theorem}[Containment of lifted sequential fusion]\label{thm:containment}
Fix $H$, $G$, a grid value $g\in\{g_1,\ldots,g_K\}$ and the mapper-derived
constants, and let $k^\star$ satisfy $g_{k^\star}=g$. The map
\begin{equation}
\iota:\mathcal F^{\mathrm{lift}}_S(H,G)\hookrightarrow\mathcal P_V(H,G),\qquad
\iota(y,p,p^W,T)=\bigl(y,\tilde p,p^W,r=0,h_{\mathrm{GM}}=e_{k^\star},T\bigr),
\label{eq:iota}
\end{equation}
with $\tilde p_{et}=1$ exactly on the timesteps covered by the pipeline's
consecutive fusion region for the edge supplying $e$ and zero elsewhere, is well defined and
injective, and it preserves the objective:
$\mathbf c^\top\iota(x)=\mathbf c^\top x$ for every
$x\in\mathcal F^{\mathrm{lift}}_S(H,G)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Feasibility.} The integer cube embeds into the LP cube,
$\{0,1\}^d\subset[0,1]^d$, so any integer-feasible $(y,p,p^W)$ lies in
$\mathcal P_V$ along the shared coordinates. The lifted $\tilde p$ is zero off
the pipeline's consecutive fusion region by construction, so the
activation-persistence and capacity rows of $\mathcal P_V$ reduce to the
pipeline's. Setting $r=0$ disables
rematerialization and $h_{\mathrm{GM}}=e_{k^\star}$ pins the buffer-capacity row
to the pipeline's $g$. The per-stage compute floor evaluates identically because both
sets consume the same mapper cost vectors under the singleton-$y$ projection.
Hence $\iota(x)\in\mathcal P_V$.
\emph{Injectivity.} $\iota$ is the identity on the shared coordinates and
constant on the new ones, so $\iota(x)=\iota(x')$ implies $x=x'$.
\emph{Objective preservation.} The objective $\sum_iT_i$ depends only on the
shared coordinate $T$, so $\mathbf c^\top\iota(x)=\mathbf c^\top x$. If the
objective additionally carries an $\varepsilon$-tiebreaker
$\varepsilon\sum_kk\,h_{\mathrm{GM},k}$, that term equals the constant
$\varepsilon k^\star$ on $\iota(x)$ and changes neither the optimum nor its
minimizer at fixed $g$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The relaxation dominates the lifted sequential objective]\label{cor:optimum}
For every chip $H$, workload $G$ and matched mapper constants,
\begin{equation}
\min_{x\in\mathcal P_V(H,G)}\mathbf c^\top x\ \le\
\min_{x\in\mathcal F^{\mathrm{lift}}_S(H,G)}\mathbf c^\top x .
\label{eq:dominates}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Let $x^\star_S$ attain the lifted sequential minimum. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:containment}, $\iota(x^\star_S)\in\mathcal P_V$ and
$\mathbf c^\top\iota(x^\star_S)=\mathbf c^\top x^\star_S$, so the minimum over
$\mathcal P_V$ is at most that value.
\end{proof}

Consequently a fixed-hardware gap between a sequential pipeline and CHEETAH
cannot be attributed to the pipeline's solutions lying outside the search space:
it must come from
the extra continuous degrees of freedom $y$, $r$ and $h_{\mathrm{GM}}$, from
relaxation versus integral rounding, or from differences in cost model or
baseline normalization.

\begin{remark}[Scope]\label{rem:scope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:containment} is a modeling-containment result and bounds the
relaxation, not the rounded executable schedule, which is reported separately.
It does not predict numerical equivalence of reported speedups: those also move
with baseline normalization, simulator overheads, depthwise mapping on
$128\times128$ arrays, and batch and core conventions. Containment is also not
automatic reproduction of a published chip, since the branch and its complete
contract must be imported, and unbounded branches can retain recession
directions at zero weight.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Branch containment in the full master]\label{cor:branch_containment}
Write an included branch as
$P_b=\{x:E_bx=e_b,\ G_bx\le g_b,\ \ell_b\le x\le u_b\}$; the master homogenizes
its rows and finite boxes with $\lambda_b$. For every included branch $p$ and
every $x\in P_p$, the assignment $\lambda_p=1$, $x_p=x$, and $\lambda_b=0,x_b=0$
for $b\ne p$ is master-feasible with the same objective and hardware readout, so
$\inf_{z\in P_M}c_M^\top z\le\min_b\inf_{x\in P_b}c_b^\top x$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every active-branch row and bound becomes its original constraint, and every
inactive-branch row has zero on both sides, which satisfies each homogenized
finite box. The weights sum to one, active hardware coordinates are unchanged,
and $\sum_bc_b^\top x_b=c_p^\top x$. The argument covers fractional points as
well as integral schedules.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A Fixed-Hardware Ceiling on Any Scheduler}
\label{app:deficiency}
For a workload $G$ on a fixed chip $H$ with DRAM bandwidth $b_H$, define the
\emph{baseline-deficiency ratio}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal D(H,G)=\frac{T_{\rm naive}(H,G)}{\max\bigl(T^{\rm LB}_{\rm compute}(H,G),\,T_{\rm memory}(H,G)\bigr)},
\label{eq:deficiency}
\end{equation}
where $T_{\rm naive}=\sum_iT^{\max}_i$ is the naive streaming time,
$T^{\rm LB}_{\rm compute}=\sum_i\min_cT^{\min}_i(c)$ is the
schedule-independent per-operation compute floor, and
$T_{\rm memory}=\sum_i\mathrm{bytes}_i/b_H$ is the bandwidth floor, where
$\mathrm{bytes}_i=\min_c\bigl[Q_i(c)-\sum_kq^k_i(c)\bigr]$ is the DRAM traffic
of operation $i$ that no residency can remove: $Q_i(c)$ is its streamed traffic
under configuration $c$ and $q^k_i(c)$ the traffic credited when operand $k$ is
resident. The
denominator is the optimistic compute-plus-DRAM roofline bound~\citep{williams2009roofline,huang2024mindthegap} on the cycles any
scheduler needs to execute $G$ on $H$. Adding NoC, SRAM, vector or control
floors can only raise it and lower $\mathcal D$.
\begin{theorem}[Fixed-hardware deficiency ceiling]\label{thm:deficiency}
For every chip $H$, workload $G$ and feasible point $x\in\mathcal P_V(H,G)$,
$\mathrm{speedup}(x):=T_{\rm naive}(H,G)/\mathbf c^\top x\le\mathcal D(H,G)$;
in particular $\min_{x\in\mathcal P_V}\mathbf c^\top x\ge\max(T^{\rm LB}_{\rm compute},T_{\rm memory})$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Both floors follow from the two latency rows of the retained block
(Appendix~\ref{app:rows_retained}),
$T_i\ge\sum_cT^{\min}_i(c)\,y_{ic}$ and
$T_i\ge\sum_cT^{\max}_i(c)\,y_{ic}-\sum_{c,k}t^k_i(c)\,w_{ick}$.
\emph{Compute.} Since $\sum_cy_{ic}=1$ and $y\ge0$, the first row gives
$T_i\ge\min_cT^{\min}_i(c)$ for every feasible $y$, and summing over $i$
gives $\sum_iT_i\ge T^{\rm LB}_{\rm compute}$.
\emph{Bandwidth.} In the cost model $T^{\max}_i(c)$ is the streamed latency,
which is at least the DRAM time $Q_i(c)/b_H$, and the residency saving for
operand $k$ is its credited DRAM time, $t^k_i(c)=q^k_i(c)/b_H$. With
$0\le w_{ick}\le y_{ic}$ and $t^k_i(c)\ge0$ the second row gives
$T_i\ge\sum_cy_{ic}\,[Q_i(c)-\sum_kq^k_i(c)]/b_H\ge\min_c[Q_i(c)-\sum_kq^k_i(c)]/b_H
=\mathrm{bytes}_i/b_H$, and summing over $i$ gives
$\sum_iT_i\ge T_{\rm memory}$. Hence
$\mathbf c^\top x=\sum_iT_i\ge\max(T^{\rm LB}_{\rm compute},T_{\rm memory})$,
and dividing $T_{\rm naive}$ by both sides gives the claim.
\end{proof}
The bound is set by the physics of the declared model, not by physics outside
it. Its denominator is built from the retained LP's per-operation compute
floors and its bandwidth row, so it limits every scheduler that draws
implementations from the declared library and is costed by the declared model,
not only CHEETAH or the sequential pipelines above. Those compute floors are mapper minima and can
contain padding and mapping inefficiency (Appendix~\ref{app:mfu}), so a
ceiling for fabricated silicon would need further assumptions about unavoidable
work, compulsory traffic and implementation coverage that the theorem does not
make. Within the physics of the declared model, $\mathcal D$ measures how much
any software can extract on a given chip--workload pair. Table~\ref{tab:deficiency} evaluates it on fixed
TPU-v3 (65,536 MACs, 900\,GB/s, 32\,MiB, BF16).

\subsection{Structure and Safe Pruning}\label{app:invar}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:invariance}
For fixed graph incidence, variable domains, and candidate library, changes
only to numerical costs and bounds preserve the structural row/column pattern.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The same indexed row templates address the same coordinates. Hardware-dependent
coefficients can change their values, but changing the implementation library or
introducing different lifetimes need not preserve the pattern.
\end{proof}
This permits reuse of indexing and warm starts. A new scaling or norm bound
must still be checked when coefficients change. Pareto pruning similarly needs
componentwise dominance in every resource and interface used by the branch.
A two-axis latency plot alone is insufficient when workspace, traffic tiers,
or numerical formats differ.
