\section{Modeling Properties and Containment}
\label{sec:theory}\label{app:containment}
The following statements concern represented feasible sets. They do not imply
that a numerical solver has optimized the complete master or that arbitrary
rounding preserves feasibility.

\subsection{Bounded Products and Epigraphs}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:mccormick_exact}
For $0\le y,p\le1$, the inequalities $0\le w$, $w\le y$, $w\le p$,
and $w\ge y+p-1$ describe the convex hull of the graph $w=yp$.
If either factor is integral, these inequalities force $w=yp$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The lower envelope is $\max(0,y+p-1)$ and the upper envelope is
$\min(y,p)$~\citep{mccormick1976computability}. For $y=0$ they both force
$w=0$; for $y=1$ they force $w=p$. The cases for integral $p$ are symmetric.
\end{proof}
This is an individual-product property, not a convex-hull theorem for the
complete program. For example,
\begin{equation}
y_1=y_2=p=\tfrac12,\quad w_1=w_2=\tfrac12
\label{eq:rlt_counterexample}
\end{equation}
satisfies the separate envelopes and $y_1+y_2=1$, but violates the identity
$w_1+w_2=p$ that holds for exact products. Linking rows
\begin{equation}
\sum_c w_{ick}=p_{\mathrm{assoc}(i,k)}
\label{eq:rlt_linking}
\end{equation}
can strengthen a branch when its intended integral semantics justify them.
They must not be added to an immutable compatibility branch while claiming
containment of every original fractional point.

\begin{corollary}[Disaggregated service epigraphs]\label{cor:v3_hull}
For fixed resource costs $a_c,b_c$, the one-choice epigraph has the perspective
form $\sum_c y_c=1$, $y_c\ge0$, $t_c\ge a_cy_c$, $t_c\ge b_cy_c$,
$T\ge\sum_ct_c$. It gives $T\ge\sum_c y_c\max(a_c,b_c)$ and dominates
$T\ge\max(\sum_ca_cy_c,\sum_cb_cy_c)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For each $c$, minimizing $t_c$ gives $y_c\max(a_c,b_c)$. Summing maxima is
at least either sum separately. With a one-hot choice the two formulations
coincide. For shared variable hardware, every additional coupling must also
be disaggregated consistently; separate product envelopes alone do not prove
the convex hull of the entire HW/SW problem.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Containment of Lifted FAST Fusion}
\label{app:fast_containment}
This subsection concerns the retained joint tiling--placement block of
Section~\ref{sec:master_lp}, whose coordinates are the tile selection
$y_{ic}$, activation residency $p_{et}$, weight residency $p^W_{it}$,
rematerialization $r_{it}$, the one-hot global-buffer selector
$h_{\mathrm{GM}}\in[0,1]^K$ over a discrete grid $g_1,\ldots,g_K$, and the
per-operation latencies $T_i$ that the objective sums. Write that block's
feasible set at a fixed chip $H$ and workload graph $G$ as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal P_V(H,G)=\{x=(y,p,p^W,r,h_{\mathrm{GM}},T):A_Vx\le b_V(H,G)\},
\label{eq:pv}
\end{equation}
with $\sum_cy_{ic}=1$, $\sum_kh_{\mathrm{GM},k}=1$, all selection and residency
coordinates in $[0,1]$, $T\in\mathbb R^L_{\ge0}$, and Timeloop-derived constants
$T^{\min}_i(c),T^{\max}_i(c),t^k_i(c)$ at $C$ Pareto tile configurations per
operation.

\paragraph{The lifted FAST fusion set.}
FAST runs the mapper first, commits to one tile choice $c^{\mathrm{FAST}}_i$ per
operation, and only then solves its fusion problem over tensor pinning. To compare
the two formulations in one variable space we lift FAST's feasible set by encoding
its committed mapper choice as a singleton one-hot,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F^{\mathrm{lift}}_F(H,G)=\Bigl\{(y,p,p^W,T):
y_{i,c^{\mathrm{FAST}}_i}=1,\ y_{ic}=0\ \forall c\ne c^{\mathrm{FAST}}_i,\
\text{$p$ in FAST's consecutive fusion pattern},\ A_Fx\le b_F(H,G)\Bigr\},
\label{eq:fflift}
\end{equation}
where $A_F,b_F$ encode FAST's buffer capacity at its hard-coded $g$, its
bandwidth, and its per-stage compute floor under the fixed-$y$ projection.
FAST commits to $r=0$ and to that fixed $g$ by construction. The first
$LC+ET+LT$ columns of $A_V$ coincide with $A_F$, and both use the same
bandwidth $b$ and the same mapper cost vectors.

\begin{theorem}[Containment of lifted FAST fusion]\label{thm:containment}
Fix $H$, $G$, a grid value $g\in\{g_1,\ldots,g_K\}$ and the mapper-derived
constants, and let $k^\star$ satisfy $g_{k^\star}=g$. The map
\begin{equation}
\iota:\mathcal F^{\mathrm{lift}}_F(H,G)\hookrightarrow\mathcal P_V(H,G),\qquad
\iota(y,p,p^W,T)=\bigl(y,\tilde p,p^W,r=0,h_{\mathrm{GM}}=e_{k^\star},T\bigr),
\label{eq:iota}
\end{equation}
with $\tilde p_{et}=1$ exactly on the timesteps covered by FAST's consecutive
fusion region for the edge supplying $e$ and zero elsewhere, is well defined and
injective, and it preserves the objective:
$\mathbf c^\top\iota(x)=\mathbf c^\top x$ for every
$x\in\mathcal F^{\mathrm{lift}}_F(H,G)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\emph{Feasibility.} The integer cube embeds into the LP cube,
$\{0,1\}^d\subset[0,1]^d$, so any integer-feasible $(y,p,p^W)$ lies in
$\mathcal P_V$ along the shared coordinates. The lifted $\tilde p$ is zero off
FAST's consecutive fusion region by construction, so the activation-persistence
and capacity rows of $\mathcal P_V$ reduce to FAST's. Setting $r=0$ disables
rematerialization and $h_{\mathrm{GM}}=e_{k^\star}$ pins the buffer-capacity row
to FAST's $g$. The per-stage compute floor evaluates identically because both
sets consume the same mapper cost vectors under the singleton-$y$ projection.
Hence $\iota(x)\in\mathcal P_V$.
\emph{Injectivity.} $\iota$ is the identity on the shared coordinates and
constant on the new ones, so $\iota(x)=\iota(x')$ implies $x=x'$.
\emph{Objective preservation.} The objective $\sum_iT_i$ depends only on the
shared coordinate $T$, so $\mathbf c^\top\iota(x)=\mathbf c^\top x$. If the
objective additionally carries an $\varepsilon$-tiebreaker
$\varepsilon\sum_kk\,h_{\mathrm{GM},k}$, that term equals the constant
$\varepsilon k^\star$ on $\iota(x)$ and changes neither the optimum nor its
minimizer at fixed $g$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The relaxation dominates the lifted FAST objective]\label{cor:optimum}
For every chip $H$, workload $G$ and matched mapper constants,
\begin{equation}
\min_{x\in\mathcal P_V(H,G)}\mathbf c^\top x\ \le\
\min_{x\in\mathcal F^{\mathrm{lift}}_F(H,G)}\mathbf c^\top x .
\label{eq:dominates}
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Let $x^\star_F$ attain the lifted FAST minimum. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:containment}, $\iota(x^\star_F)\in\mathcal P_V$ and
$\mathbf c^\top\iota(x^\star_F)=\mathbf c^\top x^\star_F$, so the minimum over
$\mathcal P_V$ is at most that value.
\end{proof}

Consequently a fixed-hardware gap between FAST-style fusion and CHEETAH cannot
be attributed to FAST solutions lying outside the search space: it must come from
the extra continuous degrees of freedom $y$, $r$ and $h_{\mathrm{GM}}$, from
relaxation versus integral rounding, or from differences in cost model or
baseline normalization.

\begin{remark}[Scope]\label{rem:scope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:containment} is a modeling-containment result and bounds the
relaxation, not the rounded executable schedule, which is reported separately.
It does not predict numerical equivalence of reported speedups: those also move
with baseline normalization, simulator overheads, depthwise mapping on
$128\times128$ arrays, and batch and core conventions. Containment is also not
automatic reproduction of a published chip, since the branch and its complete
contract must be imported, and unbounded branches can retain recession
directions at zero weight.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Branch containment in the full master]\label{cor:branch_containment}
Write an included branch as
$P_b=\{x:E_bx=e_b,\ G_bx\le g_b,\ \ell_b\le x\le u_b\}$; the master homogenizes
its rows and finite boxes with $\lambda_b$. For every included branch $p$ and
every $x\in P_p$, the assignment $\lambda_p=1$, $x_p=x$, and $\lambda_b=0,x_b=0$
for $b\ne p$ is master-feasible with the same objective and hardware readout, so
$\inf_{z\in P_M}c_M^\top z\le\min_b\inf_{x\in P_b}c_b^\top x$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Every active-branch row and bound becomes its original constraint, and every
inactive-branch row has zero on both sides, which satisfies each homogenized
finite box. The weights sum to one, active hardware coordinates are unchanged,
and $\sum_bc_b^\top x_b=c_p^\top x$. The argument covers fractional points as
well as integral schedules.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Structure and Safe Pruning}\label{app:invar}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:invariance}
For fixed graph incidence, variable domains, and candidate library, changes
only to numerical costs and bounds preserve the structural row/column pattern.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The same indexed row templates address the same coordinates. Hardware-dependent
coefficients can change their values; changing the implementation library or
introducing different lifetimes need not preserve the pattern.
\end{proof}
This permits reuse of indexing and warm starts. A new scaling or norm bound
must still be checked when coefficients change. Pareto pruning similarly needs
componentwise dominance in every resource and interface used by the branch;
a two-axis latency plot alone is insufficient when workspace, traffic tiers,
or numerical formats differ.
