\section{Examples of CHEETAH Design Blueprints}\label{app:blueprints}


We provide detailed blueprints for four prefill-oriented CHEETAH designs below, Llama-3 70B and DeepSeek-V3 in two process variants each.
For each workload we give two variants: \textbf{Variant 1} (TSMC N7~\citep{wu2016iedm7nm,chang2017sram7nm,tsmc_logic_technology,europractice_tsmc7} + GDDR/LPDDR~\citep{micron2020gddr6x,jedec2021lpddr5x} commodity memory) and \textbf{Variant 2} (TSMC N5~\citep{yeap2019iedm5nm,wikichip2020tsmc5nm,tsmc_logic_technology} + HBM3~\citep{jedec2022hbm3} upgraded memory).
Within a workload, the two variants share the same PE grid, so both inherit the same compute floor under the tile-dependent joint-tile LP, and achieve the same speedups.
The variants differ only in area, TDP, and memory-subsystem cost.
Every blueprint passes the roofline check and all six pre-RTL manufacturability gates of Appendix~\ref{app:details}.

\paragraph{Reading the utilization rows.}
Three rows below --- compute utilization, fusion efficiency and $\rho_{\mathrm{comp}}$ --- are the \emph{same} recorded ratio $t_{\mathrm{floor}}/T_{\mathrm{LP}}$ under their recorded names for it, and differ only in rounding and percentage formatting.
They measure how closely the LP objective approaches the mapper-derived compute floor of \cref{eq:floor_construction}, not what fraction of the datapath's arithmetic the model occupies.
In particular they are not comparable to FAST's compute utilization, which is a fraction of peak throughput and is reported there as $0.14$ for simulated TPU-v3~\citep{zhang_fast}; \cref{app:mfu} sets out the distinction and the FLOP-counting conventions a utilization comparison would require.

\paragraph{Compute boundedness and the bandwidth choice.}
The binding floor is compute, not bandwidth, at the target workloads examined.
At the chip's peak DRAM bandwidth (range 544\,GB/s LPDDR5X (N7) to 2{,}457\,GB/s HBM3 (N5)), we measure $T_{\text{DRAM}}/T_{\text{compute}} \le 3.3 \times 10^{-3}$ on every blueprint.
Total per-forward DRAM bytes are (13B / 65B / 70B / DS-V3) $=$ (30.5 / 121.8 / 126.2 / 95.7) GB, and production inference serving amortizes these reads across many forward passes per weight load (typical batches-per-load $\ge 100$ in vLLM and TGI).
Therefore the steady-state DRAM traffic the LP charges per forward is far below the full weight tensor.
The two TSMC N7 and TSMC N5 variants presented in Subsections~\ref{app:n7} and~\ref{app:n5} aims to make the latency--cost--yield trade-off explicit.
TSMC N7's commodity memory subsystem still meets the bandwidth floor with $\ge 200\times$ headroom (the LP consumes $< 0.4\%$ of peak BW), so paying for HBM3 (TSMC N5) buys lower TDP per delivered TFLOP, not higher compute throughput.
