\section{CHEETAH Formulation for HW/SW Co-Design}
\label{sec:methods}
CHEETAH minimizes predicted workload inference latency under a declared hardware
budget. Its inputs are independent logical invocations, a finite implementation
library, and a numerical contract. Each whole-program branch specifies compatible
hardware and execution semantics, and hardware is shared across all invocations within
that branch. The same master LP formulation admits the retained joint tiling--placement
model and causal execution branches. Variable-hardware branches retain their declared physical rows and resource
envelopes. Fixed-chip wrappers additionally impose exact installed-rate ceilings.

\subsection{Pruning, Variables, and the Encoded Features}
\label{sec:v1}\label{sec:master_lp}\label{sec:v3}
\paragraph{Pareto pruning pre-pass.}
For each operation we discard infeasible tiles and configurations dominated in
every cost, traffic, workspace and interface dimension within the same
implementation contract and hardware class. Timeloop or any other native analytical
software supplies the retained coefficients once (Figure~\ref{fig:tiles}), so the
solver never calls the mapper during the search. Appendix~\ref{app:invar} states
when the dominance relation survives a change of hardware and resulting search efficiency.

Let $i\in\mathcal N$ index logical nodes, $j\in\mathcal J$ storage objects,
$e\in\mathcal E_b=\{0,\ldots,E_b-1\}$ events in branch $b$, and $\tau$ memory
tiers. An implementation $k$ covers members $S_k$ and specifies inputs,
outputs, workspace $W_{k\tau}$ and resource demands. $\mathcal K_b$ is the
finite implementation set of branch $b$, generated on demand.
Table~\ref{tab:var_digest} defines the coordinates.

The family $\mathcal B=\mathcal B_C\mathbin{\dot\cup}\mathcal B_P$ holds causal
branches and retained joint tiling--placement branches. The
objective charges causal event durations and retained operation latencies plus
rematerialization~\citep{chen2016trainingdeepnetssublinear}, with $\kappa_b,\alpha_b$ converting to ms. Each $x_b$ holds
the branch coordinates already scaled by $\lambda_b$, and right-hand sides and
bounds scale likewise. Below we suppress branch indices and write $E=E_b$.
$\mathcal R_k$ lists required object/tier pairs, $\mathcal D_k$ completed
controls, $\mathsf D^r_e$ linear resource demand, and $\mathsf{Cap}_{b,\tau e}$
live objects, incoming copies, active outputs, workspace and reservations.
$B_b,J_b$ hold the remaining state, backing and implementation rows
(Appendix~\ref{app:master_rows} and Table~\ref{tab:feature_scope}). For
$b\in\mathcal B_{\rm phys}\subseteq\mathcal B_C$ the installed chip $\bar h_b$
fixes rates $\bar R_{br}$ and $\mathsf D^{r,\rm phys}_{be}$ is the issued work or
traffic on resource $r$ at event $e$, while $\mathsf A_b^\lambda$ and
$\mathsf P_b^\lambda$ denote affine area and peak power.

The cover, lifecycle, service and $B_b,J_b$ rows apply to causal branches, and
$A_b^P,G_b^P$ to retained schedule branches. $\mathcal W,\mathcal R,\mathcal M$
are linear workspace-product, reciprocal and service-product envelopes. Copy,
conversion, KV restoration and permitted repeat costs enter the shared service
demands and hence $d_e$, so the event epigraph already represents their overlap.
The objective minimizes latency over the declared task boundary, not area,
energy or Perf/TDP. Setting $\lambda_b=1$ recovers that branch exactly
(Corollary~\ref{cor:branch_containment}), fractional weights describe the LP
relaxation, and recovery selects one program.

\subsection{Features and Their Mathematical Encoding}
\label{sec:v3_levers}\label{sec:v0}
% \paragraph{Joint tiling and fusion.}
\paragraph{Joint tiling and fusion~\lprefobj{2,15}.}
For an isolated operation, the cover row reduces to $\sum_c y_{i,c}=1$.
A tile's traffic savings depend on residency. In the retained schedule block,
$w_{ick}$ represents $y_{ic}p_{\mathrm{assoc}(i,k),o(i)}$ through
\begin{equation}
0\le w_{ick},\quad w_{ick}\le y_{ic},\quad w_{ick}\le p,\quad
w_{ick}\ge y_{ic}+p-1.
\label{eq:mccormick}
\end{equation}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:mccormick_exact} these McCormick inequalities give the
convex hull of one bounded bilinear graph and fix the product when either
binary-domain factor is integral~\citep{mccormick1976computability}. They do not
make every fractional solution executable, and
Appendix~\ref{app:bounded_products} gives a fractional point satisfying each separate
envelope while violating the exact-product identity. Tile-dependent footprints use the
same construction. The displayed unit-bound rows are for a branch at weight
one, and homogenization replaces $1$ by $\lambda_b$. Native fused atoms carry their complete input,
output, workspace and service recipe, so fusion cannot silently remove work.
Appendix~\ref{app:tiling} discusses two detailed examples of solved instances in which a locally slower tile yields globally faster inference.

% \paragraph{Shared hardware and resource epigraphs.}
\paragraph{Shared hardware and resource epigraphs~\lprefobj{10,11,14,16}.}
Compute, vector lanes, SRAM, and delivered memory service are shared decisions
under linear area/power proxies $F_bh_b\le g_b$. Finite implementation or
technology menus use disaggregated copies with linking equalities and scaled
bounds. Resource epigraphs charge the maximum of concurrent service demands,
while demands on the same port add. Corollary~\ref{cor:v3_hull} shows the
disaggregated perspective form dominates the aggregated maximum-of-sums
alternative. Native rows aggregate work and transfer
demands per event before applying the service envelopes. Arbitrary executable
overlap is not inferred from fractional sharing. Native replay uses its
declared inter-kernel ordering and internal panel pipelines.
\label{eq:v3_caps}\label{eq:v3_epigraph}

% \paragraph{Hard rooflines and physical budgets.}
\paragraph{Hard rooflines and physical budgets~\lpref{12,13,14}.}
The strengthened fixed-chip branches enforce resource ceilings \emph{inside the LP}:
$\sum_k F_k^{\rm issued}w_{ke}\le\bar C_b d_e$ for matrix work, and
$\mathsf D^{r,\rm phys}_{be}\le\bar R_{br}d_e$ for shared HBM, host, vector,
DMA-staging and SRAM-bank-port service. Padded issued work bounds useful work, and
simultaneous kernels and copies share the same supply. Summing over events
therefore gives MFU and accounted MBU at most one. Affine installed-component
costs enforce area $\le600\,\mathrm{mm}^2$, peak power $\le300\,\mathrm W$ and
chip-average density $\le2\,\mathrm{W/mm}^2$, without discounting peak power by MFU.
The same identified template supplies combined host/HBM pin, controller,
whole-bank capacity/count/aspect, logic-depth and NoC-size constraints in
$F_bh_b\le g_b\lambda_b$. Fixed rates keep these ceilings linear. A
variable-rate McCormick envelope alone cannot enforce the exact physical
product. These are hard limits of the declared model, with the same
predicates used in recovery. Aggregate bank and NoC proxies do not prove
conflict-free placement. Appendix~\ref{app:physical_rooflines} gives
the rows and distinguishes their current implementation from the frozen certificate.

% \paragraph{Reciprocal and product envelopes.}
\paragraph{Reciprocal and product envelopes~\lpref{8,10}.}
For a rate $r\in[\ell,h]$, $\mathcal R$ bounds $u\approx1/r$ by tangents
$u\ge2/a-r/a^2$ and the global secant $u\le(\ell+h-r)/(\ell h)$.
$\mathcal M$ bounds $Z\approx Qu$ by four McCormick inequalities on
$[0,\bar Q]\times[1/h,1/\ell]$. All coefficients are constants, so the program
is linear. Outward coefficient rounding preserves graph containment.
These relax physical service, and replay reinstates the exact rates
and products. Selecting a discrete bandwidth fixes that rate, but continuous-rate
envelopes remain relaxations even when implementation selectors are integral.

% \paragraph{Time-indexed placement and KV across steps.}
\paragraph{Time-indexed placement and KV across steps~\lpref{7,9}.}
Residency $p_{j\tau e}$ changes with events rather than being fixed for the whole
run. KV production creates immutable append objects, and each decode attention
operation depends on its required prefix. The state rows preserve earlier
entries while the prefix grows. Capacity accounts for resident data, incoming
copies, and active workspace simultaneously. Long skip connections and
multi-fanout tensors use the same object identities and consumer readiness.

% \paragraph{Spill, restoration, and rematerialization.}
\paragraph{Spill, restoration, and rematerialization~\lprefobj{7,14}.}
Copies retain a valid source, reserve their destination, consume service, and
become readable only after completion. Boundary rows require the declared
terminal backing. A permitted repeat executes the original producer again and
pays its work, input and workspace demands. It restores storage without
advancing primary logical completion twice. Keeping full KV history in a
slower tier is therefore different from deleting history. The native results
credit paid KV restoration and retention. No native recomputation gain or lossy
KV budget is claimed.

% \paragraph{Weight residency and expert supply.}
\paragraph{Weight residency and expert supply~\lpref{5,9}.}
A weight tensor is an immutable object with its own residency trajectory
$p_{j\tau e}$, so retaining it across uses and paying a reload are different
points of the same rows rather than separate models. First loads and every
kernel read stay charged, and only repeated imports of a retained tensor disappear.
Capacity couples this to activation placement through the shared
$\mathsf{Cap}_{b,\tau e}$ row, so weight retention competes with KV and live
activations for the same tier. Expert weights use the identical object
identities under the declared routing model: which experts are resident versus
fetched is a residency choice subject to storage capacity, and a retained
expert still pays its first import and its per-use reads.

% \paragraph{Batching and causal scheduling.}
% \paragraph{Batching and causal scheduling~\lpref{2,3,4,5,6,8}.}
\paragraph{Batching, cover and scheduling~\lpref{1,14}.}
A batch column covers several compatible invocations and loads shared parameters
once where its recipe permits. Cover constraints retain every member, and
start/finish and readiness rows prevent using future tokens or expert selections
before their router dependencies complete. Event placement can vary within the
finite library. The current native execution uses a recovered serial schedule.
A larger scheduling domain does not imply that its optimum has been found.

% \paragraph{Implementation menus and recovery.}
\paragraph{Implementation menus and recovery~\lpref{1,14}.}
Parameter staging has explicit receive, occupancy and drain states. Full-K
panels and FP8-to-BF16 receiver implementations pay buffer, port and conversion
costs. Selected-reader backing keeps the raw or converted representation needed
by each consumer and cannot discard both. These rows connect hardware provisioning
to software reuse.
Precision, sparsity, array aspect, memory technology, and validated overlap
policies enter as implementation choices with their own numerical and resource
contracts. Accuracy-changing cache eviction or approximate reuse needs separate
quality evidence and is disabled in the full-input result. Larger
cross-GEMM fusion and unrestricted overlap have no credited gain there.
Recovery pins hardware and mappings, schedules transfers, and replays the
original obligations. The resulting chip and schedule have a predicted latency
under the analytical model. Section~\ref{sec:ocs} distinguishes hardware
branch-and-bound, continuous LP certification, and integral recovery.

% \paragraph{Serving and phase choice.}
\paragraph{Serving and phase choice~\lpref{6}.}
Prefill and decode are the same master over different event sequences and
boundary rows, not two models. A decode step inherits the KV prefix its
attention operations require, and the declared task boundary fixes which
preparation and loading costs the objective charges. Phase latencies are
therefore comparable only under one declared boundary, and the selected chip
is shared across every phase represented in a branch.

\subsection{LLM Architect Diagnostic Outer Search}
\label{sec:llm_outer}
The LLM architect reads a ledger of resource prices, slacks, invalid trials,
and replay outcomes, then proposes candidate implementations or search
neighborhoods. Deterministic validation checks each proposal before evaluation.
The architect is conditioned on a \emph{deployment profile}, a natural-language
statement of what the deployment values, because the right chip depends on it:
an overnight financial-analysis workload values sustained throughput and memory
bandwidth and tolerates latency and power, whereas streaming speech inference
values single-request latency at small batch and moderate power. The profile
sets the objective mode and steers which implementations and hardware regions
the architect proposes. Appendix~\ref{app:profiles} runs both profiles and
reports the resulting best chips (Table~\ref{tab:profile_best_chips}) and
invalid-trial rates (Table~\ref{tab:profile_invalid_rates}).
The outer loop uses Vizier~\citep{google_vizier} to search hardware
neighborhoods, while the master also represents hardware choices directly.
Dual prices guide search,
but an LLM proposal is not a complete pricing oracle: a full-domain optimality
claim requires checking all omitted alternatives. The controller and
prompts are documented in Appendix~\ref{app:llm_loop}: the prompts in
Appendix~\ref{app:prompt} and the price and validation signals in
Appendix~\ref{app:llm_duals}.
