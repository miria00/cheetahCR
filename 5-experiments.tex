\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
We evaluate predicted latency, search cost, and recovered schedules. Studies include prefill/decode and fixed-hardware mapping. For example, the full-input DeepSeek case
adds autoregressive decode and phase-specific controls. TPU-v3 provides a
publicly documented reference baseline consistent with prior
work~\citep{jouppi2021ten,zhang_fast}. All speedups below are analytical and modeled results.

\paragraph{What a new paradigm should be judged on.}
A sequential pipeline returns a design. CHEETAH returns a design \emph{and} a
certified bound \emph{and} a gate record: every reported chip comes with an LP
lower bound on any design in its declared family, passes six pre-RTL
feasibility gates whose constants carry an audited provenance trail, and sits in
a decision space whose coverage is provable (Table~\ref{tab:capability-matrix},
Theorem~\ref{thm:containment}). Raw speedup over a fixed baseline is the old
paradigm's criterion; the results below should be read against these three as
well.

\paragraph{Software alone is physics-capped; co-design is the only route past it.}
Our first result is a ceiling, not a speedup. For a fixed chip, no scheduler of
any kind can beat the ratio of the naive streaming time to the
compute-or-memory roofline floor (Theorem~\ref{thm:deficiency},
Appendix~\ref{app:deficiency}). On fixed TPU-v3 that ceiling is $2.14\times$,
$2.14\times$, $2.51\times$ and $2.22\times$ for Llama-1 13B, Llama-1 65B,
Llama-3 70B and DeepSeek-V3, and CHEETAH's fixed-hardware solutions reach
exactly $100\%$ of it on all four (Table~\ref{tab:deficiency}; three of the
four also appear in Table~\ref{tab:fixed_hw}). The modest fixed-hardware gains
are therefore not a weakness of the method but the physics of the chip:
software can recover at most about $2.5\times$ on TPU-v3, so the only route to
the $10$--$25\times$ reported below is to co-design the chip, which is what the
unified formulation enables and what a sequential pipeline cannot do. The
two-factor decomposition of Appendix~\ref{app:deficit:twofactor} makes the
split explicit for the prefill-oriented designs: of Llama-3 70B's
$40.1\times$, $1.48\times$ comes from scheduling at fixed TPU-v3 silicon and
$26.85\times$ from MAC provisioning, and the reconstructed product matches the
headline to within $1\%$ on all four LLMs, so the gain is not an artifact of
simply adding compute.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_intro}
The suite comprises EfficientNet-B1--B7, Llama-1 13B/65B,
Llama-3 70B, and DeepSeek-V3~\citep{tan2019efficientnet,llama3modelcard,deepseekv3report} and Stable Diffusion~1.5 UNet.
DeepSeek-V3 is evaluated on thirty-two 4,096-token Wikitext-2 prompts, each
decoded greedily for 512 tokens, with FP8 weights dequantized to BF16.
We compare static pinning, fixed-tile temporal
placement, and joint tiling under the same cost convention. These are restricted
controls of CHEETAH, not separate proposed systems. Small programs are solved
directly with HiGHS~\citep{huangfu2018parallel}; the native programs use OCS
(Section~\ref{sec:ocs}), whose closing fits also use HiGHS as the inner LP
engine. The expanded-master certificate composes 45,085 such fits with
original-source checks and complete branch composition. These are closing-stage counts, not the cost
of all preceding discovery runs.
The decoder study uses OCS node relaxations inside finite
hardware/software branch-and-bound: 19 node initializations and 39,000
completed updates, with zero CPU numerical LP calls. Hardware remains shared
across each recovered execution, with independent compatible implementation
choices. Exact finite-family checks are separate from numerical convergence:
the selected DeepSeek seed is 63.988923~s, while the final six-branch numerical
lower is 2.698689~s (95.782568\% gap, two open nodes). The independent exact
family check closes at the seed; neither result closes the unrestricted master.
The earlier October certificate above retains its own frozen domain.
The full-input experiment binds actual checkpoint routes and preserves every
logical invocation (Appendix~\ref{app:native_provenance}).

The current phase summary uses the unchanged naive TPU-v3 reference: original
per-operation mapper rescaling and streaming sum. The recovered decoder
execution preserves original BF16 operands, ordered FP32 arithmetic, routing,
rounded boundaries, starting KV and paid entry obligations. The reference
retains the TPU-v3 timing/precision proxy; this is not a
precision-faithful native TPU-v3 claim. Standalone nonmatrix service and cold
preparation outside the phase boundary are not silently included in the ratios.

\paragraph{Physical Design Validation.} Since modeling simulations ultimately present a gap with real world physical performance, we validate all results through a five-level audit process: (i) Roofline Auditor, (ii) Campaign Validator, (iii) Causal Ledger, (iv) solver diagnostics, and (v) six pre-RTL gates. Each CHEETAH design must pass all five audit tiers before it is deemed feasible and reported.
Appendix~\ref{app:gates} states the six gates and their thresholds,
Appendix~\ref{app:coeff_provenance} records where every gate constant and cost
coefficient comes from, and Appendix~\ref{app:blueprints} gives the complete
blueprints of the designs that pass them, so each reported chip can be audited
from its headline speedup down to the provenance of each constant it was
checked against.

The newer decode-only points are explicitly labeled \emph{relaxed
co-designed chips}: they are not gate-feasible physical chips. Their chip-power proxies are
592.51/400.49/362.62/364.21~W for DeepSeek/13B/65B/70B, all above the
prior 345-W allowance. DeepSeek assumes 2-TiB DRAM and a 37,596 shared-pin
proxy; the dense memory packages have incomplete peak-power and pin costs.
Passing the roughly 600-mm$^2$ area proxy does not remove these qualifications.

The roofline check rejects costs below compute or memory service floors.
Table~\ref{tab:mfg_gates} lists the architectural screening thresholds. These are modeling checks, not fabricated-chip timing or packaging sign-off.
RTL and synthesis evidence is in Appendix~\ref{sec:rtl_validation}.

\subsection{Reported Efficiency Quantities}
\label{sec:reported_quantities}
Three quantities carry the efficiency claims below, and each is read off the
scheduled execution rather than assumed. Let $T$ be the duration of the
interval being reported, $C_{\mathrm{peak}}$ the peak arithmetic throughput of
the recovered chip, $b_r$ the installed delivered bandwidth of resource $r$, and
$P_{\mathrm{peak}}$ its modeled peak power.
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MFU}=\frac{F_{\mathrm{useful}}}{C_{\mathrm{peak}}T},
\qquad
\mathrm{MBU}_r=\frac{B_{r,\mathrm{read}}+B_{r,\mathrm{write}}}{b_rT},
\qquad
\mathrm{Perf}/\mathrm{TDP}=\frac{Q/T}{P_{\mathrm{peak}}}.
\label{eq:reported_quantities}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Model FLOP utilization.}
$\mathrm{MFU}$ is the fraction of the chip's arithmetic capability that the
schedule converts into useful work, where $F_{\mathrm{useful}}$ counts only the
arithmetic the model requires. It is distinct from the work the arrays actually
issue: padding, masked edge tiles and replicated operands are charged to the
machine but are not useful, so $F^{\mathrm{useful}}\le F^{\mathrm{issued}}$
always. Within a fixed-chip branch the per-event service rows enforce
$\sum_i F^{\mathrm{issued}}_{i,e}x_{i,e}\le C_{\mathrm{peak}}\Delta t_e$, which
sums every concurrent implementation sharing the arrays in event $e$; because
the installed rate is a branch constant there, summing over the event intervals
guarantees an aggregate $\mathrm{MFU}\le 1$ by construction rather than by
inspection. This guarantee is a property of the fixed-chip rows and is not
inherited by the variable-hardware relaxation, where the corresponding product
is bilinear and its McCormick envelope is necessary but not exact
(\cref{app:physical_rooflines}); the installed-rate condition is therefore
re-checked exactly at execution admission.

\paragraph{Model bandwidth utilization.}
$\mathrm{MBU}_r$ is the same ratio for a memory resource, and it is computed
from the \emph{complete} scheduled traffic: operand rereads, KV transfers
between timesteps, and spill and restore movement all count, since each
occupies the interface whether or not it advances the model's arithmetic.
Because the resources have different peak rates and different consequences for
a schedule, HBM, the host link and on-chip SRAM are reported separately rather
than aggregated into a single figure.

\paragraph{Performance per TDP.}
$\mathrm{Perf}/\mathrm{TDP}$ divides delivered throughput, $Q$ units of work
per unit time, by modeled peak power. Three conditions govern its use as a
comparison. The process, the power scope and the unit of work must match
between the designs compared; a ratio across differing scopes is not a ratio of
efficiencies. Average measured power is not a substitute for modeled peak TDP,
and we do not interchange them. And because the objective minimizes latency
rather than this ratio, a performance/power tradeoff is obtained by solving
across power budgets and comparing recovered designs: for a fixed amount of
work the ratio factors as
$(\mathrm{Perf}/\mathrm{TDP})_{\mathrm{design}}/(\mathrm{Perf}/\mathrm{TDP})_{\mathrm{baseline}}
=(T_{\mathrm{baseline}}/T_{\mathrm{design}})\cdot(\mathrm{TDP}_{\mathrm{baseline}}/\mathrm{TDP}_{\mathrm{design}})$,
so a latency objective does not by itself maximize it.

For the current decode designs, performance per TDP uses a chip-power
proxy because complete package TDP is unavailable. Matched prefill and
whole-task efficiency triples have not been re-established for these
decode-only chips, so no such values or cross-phase averages are claimed.
Fractional objective bounds, replayed executions and physical qualification
remain distinct.

\subsection{Search Cost and LLM Guidance}
\label{sec:breakdown}
Cached implementation coefficients remove repeated mapper calls from the inner
search. In the 50-trial comparison, joint tiling reduces Llama-70B
search time from $58.42$ to $1.17$ hours and DeepSeek-V3 from $69.39$ to $2.17$
hours relative to the fixed-tile temporal pipeline (Figure~\ref{fig:wallclock}). These are search times,
not inference speedups. LLM guidance reduces mean invalid trials
from $24.5\%$ to $5.9\%$ while reaching the same best observed Perf/TDP score
as its full-space Vizier control (Figure~\ref{fig:llm}); Appendix~\ref{app:profiles} repeats the
search under a throughput-oriented and a latency-oriented deployment profile.
This tests proposal efficiency, not proof of
an optimal architecture or causal validity of every dual suggestion. Whether the
LLM outperforms a rule-based controller driven by the same duals and failure
classes is not established here; we therefore present the architect as a search
convenience and let the formulation and its certificates carry the contribution.
