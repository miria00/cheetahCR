\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
We evaluate predicted latency, search cost, and recovered schedules. Studies include prefill/decode and fixed-hardware mapping. For example, the full-input DeepSeek case
adds autoregressive decode and phase-specific controls. TPU-v3 provides a
publicly documented reference baseline consistent with prior
work~\citep{jouppi2021ten,zhang_fast}. All speedups below are analytical and modeled results.

\paragraph{What a new paradigm should be judged on.}
A sequential pipeline returns a design. CHEETAH returns a design \emph{and} a
certified bound \emph{and} a gate record: every reported chip comes with an LP
lower bound on any design in its declared family, passes six pre-RTL
feasibility gates whose constants carry an audited provenance trail, and sits in
a decision space whose coverage is provable (Table~\ref{tab:capability-matrix},
Theorem~\ref{thm:containment}). Raw speedup over a fixed baseline is the old
paradigm's criterion. The results below should be read against these three as
well.

\paragraph{Software alone is physics-capped; co-design is the only route past it.}
Our first result is a ceiling, not a speedup. For a fixed chip, no scheduler of
any kind can beat the ratio of the naive streaming time to the
compute-or-memory roofline floor (Theorem~\ref{thm:deficiency},
Appendix~\ref{app:deficiency}). On fixed TPU-v3 that ceiling is $2.14\times$,
$2.14\times$, $2.51\times$ and $2.22\times$ for Llama-1 13B, Llama-1 65B,
Llama-3 70B and DeepSeek-V3, and CHEETAH's fixed-hardware solutions reach
exactly $100\%$ of it on all four (Table~\ref{tab:deficiency}; three of the
four also appear in Table~\ref{tab:fixed_hw}). The modest fixed-hardware gains
are therefore not a weakness of the method but the physics of the chip:
software can recover at most about $2.5\times$ on TPU-v3, so the only route to
the $10$--$25\times$ reported below is to co-design the chip, which is what the
unified formulation enables and what a sequential pipeline cannot do. The
two-factor decomposition of Appendix~\ref{app:deficit:twofactor} makes the
split explicit for the prefill-oriented designs: of Llama-3 70B's
$40.1\times$, $1.48\times$ comes from scheduling at fixed TPU-v3 silicon and
$26.85\times$ from MAC provisioning, and the reconstructed product matches the
headline to within $1\%$ on all four LLMs, so the gain is not an artifact of
simply adding compute.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:exp_intro}
The suite comprises EfficientNet-B1--B7, Llama-1 13B/65B, Llama-3 70B,
DeepSeek-V3~\citep{tan2019efficientnet,llama3modelcard,deepseekv3report} and
Stable Diffusion~1.5 UNet. DeepSeek-V3 is evaluated on thirty-two 4,096-token
Wikitext-2 prompts, each decoded greedily for 512 tokens, with FP8 weights
dequantized to BF16. We compare static pinning, fixed-tile temporal placement
and joint tiling under one cost convention. These are restricted controls of
CHEETAH, not separate systems. Small programs are solved directly with
HiGHS~\citep{huangfu2018parallel}, and the native programs with OCS
(Section~\ref{sec:ocs}), whose closing fits use HiGHS as the inner LP engine.
Hardware is shared across each recovered execution with independent
implementation choices, and the full-input experiment binds actual checkpoint
routes and preserves every logical invocation
(Appendix~\ref{app:native_provenance}). All ratios use the naive TPU-v3
reference with its original per-operation mapper rescaling, streaming sum and
timing/precision proxy. The recovered decoder preserves BF16 operands, ordered
FP32 arithmetic, routing and paid entry obligations, and standalone nonmatrix
service outside the phase boundary is not silently included.

\paragraph{Physical Design Validation.} Since modeling simulations ultimately present a gap with real world physical performance, we validate all results through a five-level audit process (Appendix~\ref{app:validation}): (i) Roofline Auditor, (ii) Campaign Validator, (iii) Causal Ledger, (iv) solver diagnostics, and (v) six pre-RTL gates. Each CHEETAH design must pass all five audit tiers before it is deemed feasible and reported.
Appendix~\ref{app:gates} states the six gates and their thresholds,
Appendix~\ref{app:coeff_provenance} records where every gate constant and cost
coefficient comes from, and Appendix~\ref{app:blueprints} gives the complete
blueprints of the designs that pass them, so each reported chip can be audited
from its headline speedup down to the provenance of each constant it was
checked against.

The roofline check rejects costs below compute or memory service floors.
Table~\ref{tab:mfg_gates} lists the architectural screening thresholds. These are modeling checks, not fabricated-chip timing or packaging sign-off.
RTL and synthesis evidence is in Appendix~\ref{sec:rtl_validation}: BF16/FP32
decoder tests (\ref{app:wo24_decoder_rtl}), schedule-tape and INT8 datapath
checks (\ref{app:tape_checks}), post-synthesis area (\ref{sec:asap7}) and
measured energy coefficients (\ref{sec:energy_coeff}).

\subsection{Search Cost and LLM Guidance}
\label{sec:breakdown}
Cached implementation coefficients remove repeated mapper calls from the inner
search. In the 50-trial comparison, joint tiling reduces Llama-70B
search time from $58.42$ to $1.17$ hours and DeepSeek-V3 from $69.39$ to $2.17$
hours relative to the fixed-tile temporal pipeline (Figure~\ref{fig:wallclock}; Appendix~\ref{app:solver} gives the hardware
search space in \ref{app:vizier_search} and Appendix~\ref{append::lp_perform}
the LP sizes). These are search times,
not inference speedups. LLM guidance reduces mean invalid trials
from $24.5\%$ to $5.9\%$ while reaching the same best observed Perf/TDP score
as its full-space Vizier control (Figure~\ref{fig:llm}); Appendix~\ref{app:profiles} repeats the
search under a throughput-oriented and a latency-oriented deployment profile.
This tests proposal efficiency, not proof of
an optimal architecture or causal validity of every dual suggestion. Whether the
LLM outperforms a rule-based controller driven by the same duals and failure
classes is not established here; we therefore present the architect as a search
convenience and let the formulation and its certificates carry the contribution.

\paragraph{Hardware--software interaction.}
Hardware and software gains are not independent. Holding either fixed and
releasing the other recovers most of its benefit, but the combined saving is
slightly less than the sum: a wider delivery path removes some of the traffic
that better placement would otherwise have saved, so the two must be
co-selected rather than optimized in sequence. Adding on-chip delivery and SRAM
is what lets storage fusion keep intermediates on chip and lets a wider mapping
menu be used at all; a larger menu buys nothing while the execution is
service-bound. The two model families pull the chip in different directions.
DeepSeek-V3's gains come overwhelmingly from placement and residency, so its
design favors a deep memory hierarchy, with a large backing tier and capacity
for resident experts and KV, over raw delivery; the dense Llamas are
bandwidth-bound, so their designs favor off-chip and on-chip delivery and gain
least from added arrays. Within the Llama family the balance shifts with scale:
as parameters grow, keeping weights and KV resident accounts for a growing share
of the gain, so larger models favor capacity over delivery.
Table~\ref{tab:current_features} (Appendix~\ref{app:rows_scope}) records which
decision families each decoder design actually exercises. These finite-family
controls do not certify unrestricted placement, rematerialization or scheduling
optima.

\paragraph{Full-inference DeepSeek-V3.}
Appendix~\ref{sec:decode_decomp} evaluates the complete 32-request task, 4,096
prompt and 512 output tokens each, against the vanilla simulated TPU-v3
reference: the recovered chip predicts $5.25\times$ end to end, with decode
contributing $80.45\%$ of the time saved. Appendix~\ref{sec:native_features}
removes one mechanism at a time on that chip to isolate batching, storage
fusion, KV retention and time-indexed placement.
