\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
CHEETAH presents a new paradigm for hardware--software co-design: a single
master LP relaxation that unifies hardware sizing, implementation and tiling,
fusion, time-indexed placement, weight and KV residency, rematerialization and
scheduling, decision spaces that prior methods cover only in part
(Table~\ref{tab:capability-matrix}), with a containment theorem showing that
it subsumes rather than approximates the fusion search it generalizes.
Branch and bound over the declared family, compact numerical fits and
complete-domain certificates connect the relaxation to concrete designs: the
frozen 1,557-branch DeepSeek master is solved to a certified $0.067154\%$
objective gap, and every reported chip passes six pre-RTL feasibility gates
whose constants carry an audited provenance trail. We believe this is a
promising framework, and much work remains (Appendix~\ref{app:limitations}). The reported speedups are
predictions of an analytical model, not measured silicon; the recovered chips
are replayed designs rather than certified discrete optima; cross-GEMM fusion,
unrestricted overlap and the LLM architect's advantage over simpler controllers
are not yet established; and calibrating the cost model against fabricated
hardware, automating support discovery for arbitrary instances, and closing the
gap between the continuous certificate and discrete optimality are open
directions we hope this formulation makes tractable.
