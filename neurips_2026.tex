\begin{abstract}
Deep learning inference demand is projected to grow $10{,}000\times$ over the next five years, a trajectory that general-purpose accelerators cannot match. Custom accelerators offer a path forward, but designing them requires co-optimizing hardware datapaths, software schedules, and compiler transforms across a search space exceeding $\mathcal O(10^{2300})$. Existing methodologies rely on decoupled, sequential pipelines that miss critical cross-stage trade-offs. We introduce \textsc{CHEETAH}: a globally optimized, AI-driven framework for inference accelerator co-design, and the first to combine \textit{joint} tiling-fusion, dynamic placement, and rematerialization in a single linear programming (LP) formulation. Key contributions include: (1) time-indexed variables for dynamic memory management, (2) rematerialization variables that trade cheap recomputation for costly DRAM reloads, and (3) joint tiling-fusion optimization via McCormick linearization. An LLM-guided outer loop steers this inner loop through causal interventions based on deployment profiles and Lagrangian sensitivity signals. In under 6 hours, \textsc{CHEETAH} discovers Pareto-frontier designs across $12$ workloads spanning CNNs, diffusion, and dense and mixture-of-experts LLMs, reaching up to $\sim11.85\times$ speedup on vision, $\sim24.20\times$ on LLM prefill, and $\sim17.93\times$ on LLM decode over a simulated TPU-v3 baseline, with a certified $0.07\%$ optimality gap on the largest instance, unlocking efficiency inaccessible to sequential pipelines.
\end{abstract}
