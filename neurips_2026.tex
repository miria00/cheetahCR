\begin{abstract}
Inference demand is projected to grow $10{,}000\times$ in five years, motivating custom accelerators. Coordinating hardware, schedules and compiler transformations creates a combinatorial design space exceeding $\mathcal O(10^{2300})$. Sequential pipelines discard alternatives before their global memory effects are known. We introduce \textsc{CHEETAH}, an LLM-guided framework built around one master LP relaxation. Time-indexed placement and rematerialization expose reuse across operations and decode steps; McCormick envelopes couple tiling with fusion; shared hardware variables allocate compute, capacity and bandwidth under area and power budgets. Disaggregated epigraphs and implementation menus encode competing service costs. An LLM architect proposes changes using resource prices and trial diagnostics, while operator-based optimization, integral recovery and replay produce designs separately from relaxation bounds. The original prefill-oriented campaign averages $22.07\times$ speedup and reaches $40.1\times$ over modeled TPU-v3 within six hours of search. A full-inference DeepSeek-V3 design passes a declared BF16 physical scenario and predicts $20.83\times$ overall against a retained host-backed reference; reproducing FAST's modeled TPU-v3 baseline remains open. Its fixed-chip software/temporal LP has a checked $0.0847\%$ objective interval. Matched controls quantify batching, storage fusion, panel reuse, KV residency and temporal placement.
\end{abstract}
