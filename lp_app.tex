








\subsection{Linear Program Structure Details}
\label{app:lps}

\paragraph{Relaxation and Integral Recovery.}\label{app:lp_relaxation}
The master is a linear relaxation of a finite implementation and hardware library.
Its canonical form is
\begin{equation}
\min_x c^Tx\qquad\text{subject to}\qquad
\ell_c\le Ax\le u_c,\quad\ell_v\le x\le u_v.
\label{eq:lp}
\end{equation}
McCormick envelopes and epigraphs retain linearity, but do not guarantee a small integrality gap.
Integral recovery selects a concrete hardware and implementation assignment and checks the resulting schedule.
Nearest-integer rounding alone is not a feasibility proof.
Appendix~\ref{app:master_rows} gives the explicit retained joint schedule rows and the expanded causal rows.

\paragraph{Matrix Structure.}\label{append::lp_perform}
\label{app:naive_fails}
Three properties of the declared problem cause stalls in general LP solvers.
\paragraph{Scale.} The problem does not fit in memory.
Llama-1 13B's root alone
is $10.1$M columns by $18.3$M rows, its full execution adds $520$M rows, and stored densely in double precision the root alone would occupy 1.48\,PB and the full execution 43.5\,PB.
The design space exceeds $10^{2300}$ branches, so the program must be generated on demand rather than built.
\paragraph{Structure.} Almost every row is a one-hot cover, an epigraph or a
McCormick envelope, with 3.4 nonzeros per row at every scale, density below $0.01\%$ and no dense block anywhere.
Rows outnumber columns three to one, so the optimal face is massively degenerate, and cost tracks degeneracy rather than size: three times the rows costs six times the time.
\paragraph{Conditioning.} Coefficients span $10^{-6}$ to $10^{15}$, and a single
reciprocal rate variable spans $80\times$ inside its own box.
Standard first-order methods plateau at a $0.999$ primal violation after 2,000 iterations and never descend, even with the chip pinned.
A certificate therefore cannot be read off floating-point iterates (Section~\ref{sec:ocs}).

For example, at $L=T=720$ the Llama-3 70B fixed-tile control has 2.07M variables, 2.33M constraints and 4.67M nonzeros, and the joint-tile instance is of the same order with edge-indexed placement variables.
Both are sparse, at about $10^{-6}$ density, with a time-banded pattern and a few wide capacity rows.
