\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}
\subsection{Sequential Design and Information Loss}
\label{sec:background_pipeline}
Accelerator design commonly proceeds from a resource budget to a hardware
template, then to operation mappings and a memory schedule. Engineers iterate
these choices using heuristics, analytical models, simulation, and compiler feedback.
Automated flows replace individual choices with search tools, but an early
commitment still limits what downstream stages can recover. A mapper that
exports only its fastest isolated tile discards alternatives that might reduce
workspace, enable fusion, or retain data for a later consumer. Joint design
keeps those alternatives available until their complete resource costs are known in a global comprehensive end-to-end design.

\subsection{Linear Relaxation and Recovery}
\label{sec:lps}
An LP minimizes a linear objective subject to linear rows and coordinate
bounds. Discrete implementation selections are relaxed to $[0,1]$; bounded
products are represented by convex envelopes; Appendix~\ref{app:containment}
states the properties these envelopes do and do not have. The resulting optimum
is a lower bound only for the domain actually covered by the formulation and its
certificate. Fractional selections need not describe a chip. Recovery chooses
integral hardware and implementations, then checks dependencies, storage,
transfers, and predicted latency. A feasible recovered design is useful even
when the remaining optimality interval is wide. Appendix~\ref{app:lps} details
relaxation and recovery (\ref{app:lp_relaxation}) and the solver path
(\ref{app:lp_solver_path}).

\subsection{Capability Comparison}
\label{sec:capability-matrix}
Table~\ref{tab:capability-matrix} compares decision spaces, rather than claiming
that every listed capability has a measured gain. CHEETAH's row describes the
master LP. Section~\ref{sec:decode_decomp} identifies the subset exercised by
the full-inference execution. The table distinguishes searched decisions from
restricted or user-specified support. DeFiNES chooses caching versus overlap
recomputation, and Fast-and-Fusiest searches dependency-respecting orders within
its fused mapspace. Gemini's fusion and order support concerns layer pipelines.
Pluto optimizes affine schedules, and Tiramisu also exposes explicit storage,
copies and redundant computation through its scheduling language. These differ
from unrestricted cost-driven temporal placement. CoSA's guarantee concerns a
per-layer proxy objective and Fast-and-Fusiest's its fixed-hardware mapspace,
but neither is a bound over a joint hardware--schedule domain. The last column
denotes one joint mathematical program rather than staged co-search, and DOSA's
qualifying program is nonconvex.
The comparison is not only descriptive: Theorem~\ref{thm:containment} in
Appendix~\ref{app:fast_containment} exhibits an injective, objective-preserving
embedding of the lifted FAST fusion set into our relaxation, so by
Corollary~\ref{cor:optimum} our bound is never worse than FAST's matched
objective and any fixed-hardware gap must come from the extra degrees of
freedom, from rounding, or from cost-model and baseline conventions rather than
from FAST points lying outside the search space.
