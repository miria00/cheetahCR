\subsection{Feature Contributions on the Same Chip}
\label{sec:native_features}
The controls retain the same chip and remove one mechanism at a time on the same numerical workload.
These are matched execution controls, not globally optimized feature-off designs.
Interacting effects are not an additive attribution of the headline speedup.

\paragraph{Batching and storage fusion.}
The no-batch control executes every original member separately and reselects its tile.
Resident weights remain resident, and each streamed member pays its own receive.
The control expands to exactly $61{,}985{,}244$ kernels and takes $2{,}999.86$ seconds, versus $2{,}100.62$ with batching.
Thus grouped execution and shared streamed-weight delivery give $1.61\times$ decode and $1.06\times$ prefill gains.
SwiGLU and elementwise storage choices remove primitive spills while retaining arithmetic, BF16 rounding, and local/vector service.
Full-K panels reuse weights across output-row tiles, paying SRAM and serialized panel waves.
Holding one tile fixed per parameter family adds $0.87$ seconds to decode, while panel boundaries, buffers and residency stay fixed.
This tests weighted decode tile adaptation, rather than every temporal choice in the master.

\paragraph{KV and time-indexed placement.}
Every arm retains complete cached decoding, growth, host backing and mandatory writes.
Optional DRAM residency selects 246 request/layer/K-or-V prefix chains and pays 125,952 copies.
A fixed whole-task partition fits 193 chains, and releasing prefill temporaries before decode permits 246.
Placement's $3.70$-second gain is included in the $15.73$-second KV gain.
These modest marginal gains reflect limited DRAM capacity and the wide custom external interface.
Independent checks replay changed copies and kernel traffic, and Appendix~\ref{app:native_provenance} records exact inputs, costs, controls and their scope.
