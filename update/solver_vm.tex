\section{Certificate-Producing Optimization and Design Recovery}
\label{sec:solver_vm}\label{sec:ocs}\label{sec:vm_solver}\label{sec:mccormick_blowup}
To solve the master we use OCS, our in-house solver framework~\citep{feng2026ocs}.
The solver is a standalone contribution: its numerical design is described in a
companion paper, the implementation is released at the cited repository, and
Appendix~\ref{app:ocs_working_path} gives its kernel and closing stage. Here we
give only what the reader needs to follow the results. OCS never forms the event-expanded matrix: it applies the constraint
map $Kx$ and its adjoint $K^Ty$ as operators, so a branch's complete decision
domain is preserved at the size of its generated incidence. Its discovery stage
runs an anchored, restarted primal--dual scheme with a diagonal variable metric
on working supports, and both primal and dual iterates converge. Its closing
stage replaces inexact iterates with compact fits whose rational reconstruction
is verified against every original row, so the resulting bound is a certificate
rather than a solver tolerance: the primal satisfies the complete original
branch, including initial and terminal state, readiness, resource and physical
rows, and the dual has its original signs and valid Lagrangian box support over
all coordinates.

The master is solved by branch and bound over its declared family. Each branch
$b$ receives a certified lower $L_b$; branches with checked fixed-cost
infeasibility, or whose bound cannot improve the incumbent, are pruned; and the
perspective composition $L=\min_bL_b$ together with the incumbent $U$ brackets
the optimum. For the frozen 1,557-branch DeepSeek master, composing the 1,787
per-branch certificates establishes
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 L\approx946{,}487.719081842\ \mathrm{ms},\qquad
 U\approx947{,}123.742096447\ \mathrm{ms},\\
 \frac{U-L}{U}\,100\%\approx0.0671531064\%,
 \end{gathered}
\]
with 1,095 branches meeting the stopping threshold, 462 pruned by fixed-cost
infeasibility and none unresolved (Appendix~\ref{app:expanded_native_certificate}).

\paragraph{Hardware branch and bound and recovery.}
Integral recovery searches a finite tree over array counts, vector lanes, DRAM
bandwidth, host channels and local-service units; optimistic resource and
capacity-priced retention bounds prune regions that are budget-infeasible or
cannot improve a replayed incumbent, and leaves use actual implementation and
capacity costs. Recovery then selects one branch, integral implementations,
hardware and storage policies, and analytical replay checks dependency
readiness, capacities and service at the selected rates. The retained execution
takes $2{,}718{,}970.508466$ ms; the continuous upper above is not its latency.
Reciprocal/product relaxation and integral recovery gaps remain, so the
continuous certificate does not establish discrete chip/schedule optimality
(Appendix~\ref{app:native_solve_workflow}).
