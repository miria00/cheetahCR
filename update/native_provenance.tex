\section{Native Full-Inference Provenance and Reproduction}
\label{app:native_provenance}
\subsection{Baseline and BF16 physical-cost reconciliation}
\label{app:native_feasibility_repair}
The native ratios below retain their original analytical contracts. They do
not yet establish comparisons against FAST's modeled TPU-v3 baseline, which
starts from ordinary XLA fusion regions on two cores with two $128\times128$
MXUs per core, 16 MiB SRAM per core, 123 BF16 TFLOP/s and 900 GB/s per chip.
FAST's additional fusion/scheduling is a separate optimized arm. Its internal
simulator and compiler artifacts are not available here; the replacement is an
explicit reconstruction with complete device residence, matrix mapping and
nonmatrix service, rather than a claim to reproduce that internal simulator.

The archived native MAC peak-power slope is 0.4 mW, whereas the appendix
specifies 1.2 mW for BF16. Replacing only that slope makes all twelve saved
chips exceed 300 W. Area, banks, pins and NoC costs also require explicit
shared assumptions. A new versioned analytical scenario applies the BF16
power slope, a shared 4,096-signal-pin budget for host and HBM traffic, and
declared SRAM port/geometry assumptions. The inherited area slope is varied
by factors of 1, 2 and 4; these are sensitivities, not calibrated BF16 bounds.
New whole-chip branches use identical constraints for LP construction and
execution admission. A passing scenario is not silicon sign-off.

For fixed chip $h$, each event satisfies total issued matrix work
$F_e\le P_h t_e$ and total accounted interface traffic $D_e\le B_h t_e$.
Summing over the same phase proves aggregate MFU and MBU at most one;
padding, concurrent work and copies cannot be omitted. Variable-hardware
McCormick points remain relaxation bounds, not physical latency predictions.
The new contract does not relabel the frozen native LP certificate.
Perf/TDP comparisons await matched process, workload and peak-power scope.

The construction and evidence are recorded in
\path{systems/cheetah_joint/audits/20261026/BASELINE_FEASIBILITY_REPAIR.md}
and \path{systems/cheetah_joint/audits/20261026/BASELINE_EXECUTION_CONTRACT.md}.

The corrected DeepSeek chip and its full-domain fixed-chip LP interval are
reported in Appendix~\ref{app:corrected_native_lp}. Its retimed workload
remains separate from the historical comparisons below.

\subsection{Archived native results}
\paragraph{Frozen input.}
DeepSeek-V3 is pinned to checkpoint revision
\texttt{e815299b0bcbac849fa540c768ef21845365c9eb}.
Thirty-two nonoverlapping validation-text windows from Wikitext-2 produce
4,096-token prompts using the pinned tokenizer. Generation is greedy with
512 outputs per request. All 61 layers and 58 routed MoE layers execute;
all route IDs and aligned weights are retained. The observation policy
dequantizes original FP8 blocks to BF16, preserves higher-precision tensors,
disables TF32 and reduced-precision BF16 accumulation, and uses the pinned
normalization/router behavior. The workload is reproducible, not a language
quality or representativeness study. Capture and solver GPU timings are not
custom-chip performance estimates.

\paragraph{Archived chip and controls.}
The earlier result uses source identity
\path{831edc6f8cde9bd2091541b55beaa00fae2736035badb3937236ba32c66104e3}
and family
\path{c2dbac2a699129be05060253c6d44292644ee7fa244f3cd9590bf5da6c113dd8}.
Its 21 branches retain prior alternatives. The 320-array execution predicts
$31.705749\times$ overall, $19.852844\times$ prefill and $37.931777\times$
decode speedup. Joint weight/KV recovery retains 281,872 weight gaps and
246 prefix chains. Its conservative execution reservation uses
68,697,653,440 of 68,719,476,736 DRAM bytes. The separately admitted continuous

The historical four-branch lower below was not transferred to this expanded
family; its new lower is derived over all 21 branches.

The archived controls use this same chip, weight plan, KV policy and causal progress.
Removing batching expands original groups into serial members inside each
original dependency envelope; prefill members retain their actual routed row
counts. Each streamed member pays a complete weight receive. Independent checks
recount 31,706,528 decode and 1,441,733 prefill matrix members, and verify every
selected receiver template. Fusion controls restore original paid primitive
spills, preserving local service and numerical boundaries. The added private
scratch is 335,872 DRAM bytes and fits beyond the retained execution pool.
Disabling full-K prefill panels reselects among the five ordinary tiles within
the original workspace allowance. All other owners, dependencies and terminal
obligations inherit the admitted execution. These controls are analytical
substitutions, not new continuous-source admissions or feature-off optima.

\paragraph{Reference sensitivity.}
The headline uses a declared 32-GB/s external link for the native TPU-v3
extension and 1,024 GB/s for the custom design. The preceding matched
interface-sensitivity study, on the pre-feature custom execution, reduces its
$22.56\times$ comparison to $4.54$--$4.80\times$ when the same naive reference
is assigned 1,024 GB/s. This is evidence that interface assumptions materially
affect the headline, not a new ratio for the final feature-enabled chip.
The original study varies vector/local service as documented in
\path{audits/20261023/NATIVE_REFERENCE_SENSITIVITY.md}.
A well-scheduled, fully calibrated TPU serving stack is not established by the
naive analytical reference.

\paragraph{Negative scheduling result.}
Two additional current-head batching policies preserve all decode invocations
and pass independent dependency checks, but their predicted costs increase by
1,829.259 and 1,840.501 seconds relative to the retained execution. They are
rejected. This motivates joint pricing of waiting, batch alignment, and parameter
reuse; it does not prove that all alternative schedules are worse.

\paragraph{Joint array and tile recovery.}
Reselecting among all five original tiles at each array endpoint improves the
518-array candidate to 2,871.252 seconds: $23.196\times$ overall,
$13.607\times$ prefill, and $28.551\times$ decode. Fifty-six expert gate/up
batch classes change tiles, covering 8,550,454 decode groups and four prefill
groups. The additional saving is 3.519 seconds, almost entirely in decode;
the 429- and 473-array candidates retain their tiles. Independent reconstruction
and original-row admission pass with unchanged KV, fusion, copies and causal
order. Every added group choice remains independently addressable. This extends
the candidate family to five branches; the four-branch lower bound above is
not transferred. The exact witness and replay are recorded in
\path{audits/20261023/NATIVE_ARRAY_TILES.md}. This is a restricted joint recovery
result, not full-master pricing or global hardware/software optimality.
